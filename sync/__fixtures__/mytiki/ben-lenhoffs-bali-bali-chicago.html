Ben Lenhoff's Bali Bali - Chicago

Chicago, Illinois, United States

Add Visit

This pre-tiki establishment was started by Ben Lenhoff some time before 1937 and was being run by him at least as late as 1937, with salvaged decor from the Chicago World's Fair (1933-1934). It apparently went through a few owners and eventually burned down in 1941.

From The Daily Calumet newspaper, March 5th, 1941:

"NIGHT CLUB DESTROYED BY FIRE

One of Southeastern Chicago's night spots, Bali Bali, 7223 South Chicago avenue, was gutted by flames this morning in a spectacular 2-11 blaze, which caused $20,000 damage and routed patrons and employees.

The fire was discovered by an unidentified patron who went to the washroom in the rear about 5 a.m. He excitedly returned telling William "Bill" Sabata, proprietor, that there was a fire. Sabata picked a pail of water and went to the rear but the blaze had already gained momentum and was beyond his control. He called South Shore police while someone notified the fire alarm office. Employees and entertainers picked up what clothing they could salvage and ran to the outside. Several of the women entertainers lost their coats in the flames. The cash box was carried out by Sabata.

Battalion Chief Thomas Logan, Engine Co. 47, truck Co. 34 and squad 5 were the first on the scene. The flames were shooting out through the roof in the back when they arrived. Chief Logan immediately ordered a box alarm turned in followed by a 2-11. Lieut. John J. McCarthy and several policemen responded and aided in directing traffic.

The blaze ate its way through the back, ignited the inflammable decorations in the barroom and dance hall and continued through to the front of the building. Scores of firemen fought the blaze for several hours before it was extinguished. The roof caved in as did the back wall and parts of the side walls. The building was a total loss.

Division Marshal John Moynihan, who also responded to the alarm, told The Daily Calumet reporter that the cause of the blaze was un-determined. One theory was that a lighted cigarette stub left in the rear may have started the fire, while another was cross wires.

The building was originally a garage and several years ago was remodeled and made into a night club. For sometime it thrived and then it changed hands many times. It is said that the present owner had a very profitable business. Much of the material used in re-modeling was salvaged from the World's Fair."
